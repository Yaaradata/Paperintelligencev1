## Per-paper seat scoring (adapt anchors here — not in the seat file)

You are scoring **each paper independently**, not picking a single winner.

### TECH — high-score meaning

A high score (8+) means the reader could plausibly say one of:

- "I can have my team try this on our stack this quarter and report back."
- "This changes an architecture / eval / cost / safety bet I am about to make."
- "I can use this to judge whether a vendor or internal claim is real."

### PRODUCT — high-score meaning

A high score (8+) means the reader could plausibly say one of:

- "This changes what we should build or how we should scope an AI feature."
- "This tells me something real about user behaviour, trust, adoption, or risk."
- "This affects go-to-market, pricing, compliance, or process design for AI."

### Anchor scale (3–6 month decision test)

For **each** seat, ask: "What decision, experiment, review, or operating change
could this reader initiate within the next 3–6 months because of this evidence?"

| Score | Meaning for that seat |
|------:|------------------------|
| **2** | Weak / stretch. No credible 3–6 month action for that seat's reader; mention of related topics without decision consequence. |
| **5** | Partial. A plausible but soft action exists (watch, discuss, bookmark); evidence or transferability is thin. |
| **8** | Strong. The reader could credibly start a test, review, or decision in 3–6 months; at least one high-score anchor sentence fits. |
| **10** | Exceptional. Clear, evidence-backed decision value for that seat; multiple anchors fit and the path is concrete. |

Scores are **independent**. Both high, one high, or both low are all valid.

### Hard rules

- **Sector mention alone does NOT raise `product_relevance`.** Naming healthcare,
  finance, mining, etc. without a product/process/governance decision stays low
  on PRODUCT (often ≤4). Tag the sector in `application_domain` instead.
- **Being understandable to a product leader is not enough** for a high
  `product_relevance`. The paper must change a plausible product, process,
  commercial, or governance decision.
- Pure theory / benchmark-only engineering novelty without implementable path
  stays low on TECH even if impressive.

## application_domain / general_method

`general_method` is **mutually exclusive** with every other application domain and is
the **correct answer for most papers**.
If you find yourself constructing an argument for why a method "could apply" to a
sector, the answer is `general_method`.
Only assign a sector when the title/abstract **explicitly** situates the work in
that sector (clinical data, trading, plant floor, grid, farm, court, SOC, etc.).

`application_domain` is metadata only — it does **not** decide tech/product pool
membership (seat scores do).
