## Audience definitions (use these literally)

Assign every label that clearly fits. Most papers get 1–2 labels; do not default
everything to `practitioner`.

| Label | Who | Assign when |
|-------|-----|-------------|
| **practitioner** | Hands-on ML/AI builders (engineers, applied researchers) | The paper changes how someone would **implement, train, evaluate, or debug** a model/system. Method, architecture, benchmark, tooling. |
| **technical_leadership** | Staff+/principal engineers, research leads | The paper changes a **technical strategy** choice (roadmap, architecture bet, eval protocol, safety posture) — not only a coding detail. |
| **enterprise_adoption** | Product, ops, risk, or business decision-makers | The paper changes a **buy / deploy / govern / expose** decision in a real organisation or market. Production deployment, ops cost, compliance, ROI, organisational risk, industry workflow. **Industry/company names are sufficient but not required.** |
| **student** | Learners | The paper is primarily pedagogical or a clear learning resource. |

Rules:
- A pure method/scaling/benchmark paper with no deployment or organisational decision → `practitioner` (and maybe `technical_leadership`), **not** `enterprise_adoption`.
- A production system, industrial case study, regulated domain deployment, security exposure decision, or ops/cost result → include `enterprise_adoption` even if it is also technically deep.
- **Do not require a company name** for `enterprise_adoption`; a concrete operational setting is enough.

## application_domain / general_method

`general_method` is **mutually exclusive** with every other application domain and is
the **correct answer for most papers**.
If you find yourself constructing an argument for why a method "could apply" to a
sector, the answer is `general_method`.
Only assign a sector when the title/abstract **explicitly** situates the work in
that sector (clinical data, trading, plant floor, grid, farm, court, SOC, etc.).
